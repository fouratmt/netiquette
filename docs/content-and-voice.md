# Content and voice guide

## Voice

Netiquette is **kind, neutral, clear, and practical**.

It should sound like a thoughtful person explaining a convention—not a judge
issuing a verdict. The reader may have made a mistake without knowing the
convention existed.

## Writing principles

### Describe the behavior, not the person

Prefer “Putting a call on speaker without warning can expose a private
conversation” over “Only rude people put calls on speaker.”

### Assume good intent

Do not suggest that a mistake proves ignorance, selfishness, age, or lack of
technical ability.

### Explain concrete impact

Name effects such as lost privacy, unexpected exposure, interruption,
embarrassment, pressure, or confusion. Avoid vague appeals to “proper behavior.”

### Offer an easy alternative

The recommendation should be specific enough to use immediately: “Ask first
and wait for an answer before turning on speakerphone.”

### Avoid absolutes when context matters

Use “usually,” “may,” or a short nuance section when conventions depend on the
relationship, platform settings, culture, accessibility needs, or safety.

### Do not weaponize the page

Avoid second-person accusations such as “You should know better.” The page may
be sent during a sensitive interaction, so it should lower the temperature.

## Languages

The product supports English, French, and Tunisian Arabic. All three versions
should communicate the same intent and level of kindness, but translations do
not need to be literal. Natural local phrasing is more important than preserving
the source language's sentence structure.

Tunisian Arabic uses Arabic script in the MVP and is presented right-to-left.
Latin-script Arabizi may be considered later if readers ask for it. Each
translation should ultimately be reviewed by a fluent speaker rather than
accepted as unreviewed machine translation.

## Entry template

The editable source is one Markdown file per behavior in
`content/etiquettes/`. Follow `content/README.md` for the GitHub web-editor
workflow and copy `content/ETIQUETTE_TEMPLATE.md` when adding a behavior. The
headings in that template must remain unchanged; the prose beneath them is the
editorial content.

Each entry should contain:

1. **Title** — a short, positive instruction.
2. **One-sentence takeaway** — the entire recommendation at a glance.
3. **Situation** — when this convention applies.
4. **Why it matters** — the effect on other people.
5. **What to do instead** — concrete steps or wording.
6. **Nuance** — exceptions or context, only when useful.
7. **Severity** — a four-level estimate of the possible impact if ignored.
8. **Category and tags** — for browsing and search.
9. **Platforms** — named services when the behavior is platform-specific.
10. **Related entries** — closely connected conventions.

Severity describes the possible impact of the behavior, never the character of
the person. Use level 1 for a small social awkwardness, level 2 for discomfort
or disruption, level 3 for a serious effect on privacy or trust, and level 4
for a clear consent, privacy, or safety risk.

The interface labels this scale as **Potential impact**, **Impact possible**,
and **التأثير الممكن**. Avoid punitive labels such as “severity of
non-compliance,” which can make a shared page sound disciplinary.

Categories describe the situation, such as calls, messaging, or privacy.
Platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger are a separate grouping
dimension. An entry can reference multiple platforms, and a general convention
does not need a platform.

## Example draft

### Ask before using speakerphone

**Takeaway:** Tell the other person and get their agreement before putting a
call on speaker.

**Situation:** You want to free your hands, let someone else join the call, or
make a call easier to hear.

**Why it matters:** The caller may reasonably believe only you can hear them.
Turning on speaker without warning can expose private information or include
other listeners without the caller’s consent.

**What to do instead:** Say why you would like to use speakerphone, mention who
else is present, and wait for the caller to agree. If they are uncomfortable,
use headphones or keep the call private.

**Nuance:** In an emergency or accessibility-related situation, explain the
change as soon as it is safe and practical.

## Vocabulary to avoid

- “Boomer,” “old people,” “technologically challenged,” or any wording that
  identifies the implicit audience by age.
- “Everyone knows,” “obviously,” or “common sense.”
- “Stalking” as a factual accusation when “browsing older posts” describes the
  behavior accurately.
- Platform claims presented as permanent facts when visibility depends on
  settings or features that may change.

## Editorial checklist

- Is the title a helpful instruction rather than an accusation?
- Can the takeaway be understood by itself?
- Does the explanation identify a real effect on someone else?
- Is the recommended alternative concrete?
- Is the rule still accurate across common platform settings?
- Are relevant accessibility, cultural, or safety exceptions acknowledged?
- Would the wording feel fair if received immediately after making the mistake?
- Does the entry avoid age-based or ability-based assumptions?
- Does each translation sound natural and preserve the same social meaning?
- Are named-platform claims current, qualified, and necessary?

## Shared-page context

The secondary context panel and footer are explanatory rather than accusatory.
They should briefly say what Netiquette is, why the site exists, and why someone
may choose to share an individual page. They should not say or imply “someone
sent you this because you behaved badly.” The wording must remain tentative
about any particular sender's intent.

The text may be visually quieter than the entry, but it must retain accessible
contrast and a readable font size. “Small” should mean secondary, not difficult
to read.
